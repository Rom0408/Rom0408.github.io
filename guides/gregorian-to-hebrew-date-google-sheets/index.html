Convert Gregorian dates to Hebrew dates in Google Sheets

Turn a column of calendar dates into Hebrew dates in Google Sheets, in Hebrew letters or English, and convert Hebrew dates back.

People ask for a Hebrew date column for invoices, yahrzeit lists and event planning. Sheets cannot do this alone, but there are two simple routes depending on how many dates you have.

1. A few dates: use the browser converter

Open the Hebrew date converter, pick the date and copy the result. This is the quickest option for ten or fewer dates.

2. A whole column: use a formula

The Sheets Cleanup & Hebrew Toolkit add-on adds GREG2HEB, which returns the Hebrew date for a real date value. Pass "he" for Hebrew letters or "en" for English transliteration.

=GREG2HEB(A2,"he")
=GREG2HEB(A2,"en")

3. Make sure your dates are real dates

If the formula returns an error, the cell probably holds text rather than a date. Check by changing the format to Number: a real date turns into a number like 45000. Text stays as it is.

4. Convert back when needed

HEB2GREG takes a Hebrew date written as text and returns the calendar date.

=HEB2GREG("ט״ו בניסן תשפ״ד")

Watch out for

A Hebrew day starts at nightfall. A formula that works on calendar days gives the Hebrew date that applies during the day. For evening events, such as a yahrzeit that begins at sunset, consider the day before.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do this with a built-in Sheets function?

No. There is no native function for Hebrew dates.

Which years are supported by the free converter?

1900 to 2100.

Skip the formula work

Free tool: Hebrew Date Converter, runs in your browser. In Google Sheets, Sheets Cleanup & Hebrew Toolkit will add the GREG2HEB and HEB2GREG functions. It isn't available yet.
