Hebrew holiday dates in Google Sheets

How Jewish holidays map to Gregorian dates, and how to get Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Sukkot, Pesach and Shavuot dates into a Google Sheet.

Google Sheets has no built-in Hebrew calendar, so holiday dates have to come from somewhere else. The good news is that every holiday sits on a fixed Hebrew date, and only the Gregorian date changes from year to year.

1. Know the Hebrew date of each holiday

Rosh Hashanah is 1 and 2 Tishrei. Yom Kippur is 10 Tishrei. Sukkot begins on 15 Tishrei. In Israel, Shemini Atzeret and Simchat Torah fall together on 22 Tishrei. Pesach begins on 15 Nisan, and Shavuot is on 6 Sivan.

2. Look up the Gregorian date

Type the Hebrew date into the free converter, for example ט״ו בניסן followed by the Hebrew year, and copy the calendar date into a column of your sheet. Repeat for each holiday you need.

3. Automate it for many years

With the Toolkit add-on you can convert a Hebrew date text to a calendar date and ask for the holiday name of any date.

=HEB2GREG("ט״ו בניסן תשפ״ד")
=HEBHOLIDAY(A2)

4. Use the dates

Once the dates sit in a column, you can feed them to workday formulas, calendars and reminders like any other date.

Watch out for

Observed dates can differ from the Hebrew date. For example, Independence Day is moved in some years when the Hebrew date falls on a Friday or Saturday, and holidays last different numbers of days in Israel and abroad. For a contract, payroll or a legal deadline, confirm with the official Israeli calendar.

Frequently asked questions

Does Google Sheets have a Hebrew calendar function?

No. Sheets has no built-in function that converts to or from the Hebrew calendar, so you need a converter or an add-on.

Why do holiday dates change every year?

The Hebrew calendar is a lunisolar calendar, so a fixed Hebrew date lands on a different Gregorian date each year.
