Remove hidden direction marks from Hebrew text in Google Sheets

Find and delete invisible left-to-right and right-to-left marks that break matching and sorting of Hebrew text in Google Sheets.

Text copied from emails, web pages and some systems can carry invisible Unicode direction marks. Two cells that look identical then fail to match, because one contains characters you cannot see.

1. Detect affected cells

Compare the length of the cell with the length after removing the marks. A difference means hidden characters.

=LEN(A2)<>LEN(REGEXREPLACE(A2,"[\x{200E}\x{200F}\x{202A}-\x{202E}\x{2066}-\x{2069}]",""))

2. Remove them

The pattern covers the left-to-right and right-to-left marks, the embedding and override controls, and the isolates.

=REGEXREPLACE(A2,"[\x{200E}\x{200F}\x{202A}-\x{202E}\x{2066}-\x{2069}]","")

3. Clean spaces too

Combine it with TRIM so that stray spaces go as well.

=TRIM(REGEXREPLACE(A2,"[\x{200E}\x{200F}\x{202A}-\x{202E}\x{2066}-\x{2069}]",""))

4. Paste values

Paste the cleaned values over the original column once you have checked a few rows.

Watch out for

Direction marks are sometimes useful. In mixed Hebrew and English text they can keep punctuation where it belongs on screen. After removing them, check how the text displays.

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell a cell has hidden marks?

The length check in the first step finds them. Clicking into the cell and moving the cursor with the arrow keys can also show an extra stop.

Is there a one-step function?

Not yet. A STRIPBIDI function is planned for the Hebrew Text Pack, coming soon. Until then, use the formula above.
